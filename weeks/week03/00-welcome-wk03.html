Why the order matters

The guided assignment is not a warm-up you can skip — it's the instruction. I do every step of it on camera, so a perfect 25 is genuinely there for the taking. Then the homework asks for the same commands on a different document, which is the part that proves you can actually do it. Doing the homework first just means struggling through something you were about to be shown.

One score in each category gets dropped. Across the semester your lowest 25-point score and your lowest 100-point project score are dropped automatically. That's your grace for a bad week — it is not a reason to skip one on purpose, because you only get the one.

How to read the book without drowning in it

The chapter is long and dense with numbered steps, and reading it cover to cover before you touch Word is a miserable way to spend an evening. It also isn't what it's for.

On the first pass, read for capability. You're building a mental list of what Word can do — that there is such a thing as a picture style, that page borders are a setting, that fonts have effects beyond bold and italic. You don't need to remember where any of them live.

Then use it as a reference. When the assignment says "apply the Soft Edge Oval picture style" and you have no idea what that means, the chapter has the exact steps with screenshots. That's the moment the step-by-step detail earns its keep.

Windows and Mac

Both work for this course. Pick your platform for what to expect.

On Windows

The book, the MindTap instructions and my walkthrough videos are all recorded on Windows, so what you see on screen is what you'll see. Nothing extra to do — just make sure you're using the desktop version of Word, not the web or phone app, since those are missing features the assignments need.

On Mac

  • Read the Word for Mac material in this module before you start. It's there specifically because the Cengage material assumes Windows.
  • Most things are the same — the ribbon tabs, the command names, the dialogs. Enough is different that you'll occasionally hunt for something; watermarks are one example.
  • Look for the Mac notes. MindTap's instructions sometimes include a short "if you have a Mac, do this instead" line. They're easy to skim past.
  • When you get stuck, message me through the Canvas Inbox with the step number and what you tried. I've worked through these assignments on a Mac myself — I may not remember the exact menu, but I can find it.
  • Use the desktop version of Word, not the web or phone app.
