Excel Project: Coastal Coffee Shop Quarterly Analysis

This is the end of the Excel unit. You take a starter workbook of raw numbers and turn it into a finished quarterly report: formulas and functions, formatting, conditional formatting, sparklines, a chart, and a loan analysis with what-if tools. There's no walkthrough this time. Every skill here is covered in the textbook and in the assignments you've already done, so if you don't remember how to do something, look it up.

  • Worth: 200 pts, not droppable
  • Due: Sun 11/15, 11:59 PM
  • Submit to: Canvas, not MindTap
  • Covers: Excel Modules 1–4
  • Late: only if arranged before the due date

Before you start

You're the assistant manager at Coastal Coffee Shop, a local café in its third year. The owner wants a report on the third quarter of 2026 (July through September): what each product category sold, which ones earned the staff bonus, and how sales trended. She's also thinking about financing a new espresso machine and wants to know what the payments would look like. The numbers are already in the starter file. Your job is everything else.

There's no MindTap module this week. The whole week is this project, and it's worth more than 10% of your grade. Plan on about two hours, and don't start it Sunday night. Veterans Day is Wednesday 11/11; the campus is closed, but the course is asynchronous, so the due date doesn't move.

The starter file

Download coastal_coffee_fall26_starter.xlsx from the Excel Project assignment in Canvas and open it in the desktop version of Excel. The web and phone versions don't have all of these commands. Select Enable Editing if Excel asks, then save your own copy right away with the file name under Saving and submitting.

I check your work cell by cell, so don't insert, delete or move any rows or columns, and don't retype the numbers. Every cell address on this page assumes the layout of the starter file.

Mac users

Everything in this project works in Excel for Mac. A few names or locations differ; where that matters, a blue note under the task says so. If you're still stuck, message me through Canvas Inbox.

The project, part by part

Each task is graded on its own, so a miss in one doesn't cost you points in another. Unless a task says otherwise, Parts 1 through 6 happen on the Q3 Sales sheet.

Open the starter file, save your own copy, then do these four things before anything else.

  1. Theme: set the workbook theme to Office 2013 – 2022. Every color named later in this project ("Blue, Accent 1", "Gold, Accent 4" and so on) comes from this theme.

    Mac: it's in the same Themes gallery. If you can't find it, message me before you go on, since the colors in later parts depend on it.

    4 pts

  2. Spelling: run spell check on Sheet1. It finds two misspelled words in column A. Correct both with Excel's suggestion.

    5 pts

  3. Rename Sheet1 to Q3 Sales.

    3 pts

  4. Tab color: make the Q3 Sales sheet tab Blue, from the Standard Colors row.

    3 pts

  1. Title: merge and center A1:H1. Apply the Title cell style, then change the font size to 20 pt.

    6 pts

  2. Subtitle: merge and center A2:H2. Make it italic with font color Green, Accent 6, Darker 50%.

    5 pts

  3. Column headings: apply the Heading 3 cell style to A4:H4 and center them.

    4 pts

  4. Section labels with Format Painter: format A12 (Quarter Statistics) as Bold, 12 pt, font color Blue, Accent 1, Darker 25%. Then use the Format Painter to copy that formatting to A17 (Bonus Program). The two labels should look identical.

    5 pts

  5. Total row: apply the Total cell style to A10:H10.

    5 pts

Every number you add must be a formula or function, never a value you worked out and typed in. I check the Formula Bar, and a typed number earns no points even when it's right.

  1. Quarter Total: in E5, use the SUM function to add July through September for Hot Beverages. Copy it down through E9.

    5 pts

  2. Monthly totals: in B10:E10, use SUM to total each column (rows 5 through 9).

    5 pts

  3. % of Total: in F5, write a formula that divides the category's Quarter Total by the grand total in E10. Use an absolute reference for E10 so the formula copies correctly, then copy it down through F9. In F10, use SUM to total F5:F9. It should come to 100%.

    8 pts

  4. Accounting format: format B5:E10 as Accounting with 0 decimal places.

    4 pts

  5. Percent format: format F5:F10 as Percent Style with 1 decimal place.

    4 pts

  6. Date prepared: in B3, use the NOW function, then format B3 as a Short Date. It will show whatever day you open the file, and that's expected.

    4 pts

The owner pays a staff bonus on any category that sells at least $15,000 in the quarter. The bonus is 5% of that category's Quarter Total. Both numbers live in B18 and B19, so she can change them later without touching a formula.

  1. Defined names: select A18:B19 and create names from the labels in the left column. You should end up with two names, Bonus_Threshold (B18) and Bonus_Rate (B19).

    5 pts

  2. Format the settings: B18 as Accounting, 0 decimal places; B19 as Percent Style, 0 decimal places (it shows 5%). Give B18:B19 a fill color of Gold, Accent 4, Lighter 60%.

    4 pts

  3. Bonus formula: in H5, use the IF function. If the Quarter Total in E5 is greater than or equal to the threshold, the result is the Quarter Total times the bonus rate. Otherwise the result is 0. Use the names Bonus_Threshold and Bonus_Rate in the formula, not B18 and B19. Copy it down through H9.

    8 pts

  4. Bonus total: in H10, use SUM to total the bonuses. Format H5:H10 as Accounting with 2 decimal places.

    3 pts

All three statistics look at the three monthly totals in B10:D10, not at the individual categories.

  1. In B13, use the MAX function to find the highest monthly total.

    4 pts

  2. In B14, use the MIN function to find the lowest monthly total.

    4 pts

  3. In B15, use the AVERAGE function to find the average monthly total.

    4 pts

  4. Format B13:B15 as Accounting with 0 decimal places.

    3 pts

The owner wants to see the strong and weak categories at a glance. Two rules go on the same range, so check that you add the second rule rather than replace the first.

  1. High sellers: on E5:E9, highlight values greater than 20000 with Light Red Fill with Dark Red Text.

    4 pts

  2. Low sellers: on the same range, E5:E9, add a second rule that highlights values less than 10000 with Yellow Fill with Dark Yellow Text. Both rules should be listed when you manage the rules for this sheet.

    4 pts

  3. Data bars: on F5:F9, add Gradient Fill data bars in Blue. Leave F10 out.

    4 pts

  4. Sparklines: in G5:G9, insert Line sparklines, one per category, showing that category's July through September sales (B5:D9).

    5 pts

  5. Markers: turn on Markers for the sparklines so each month shows as a dot.

    3 pts

One chart, comparing the five categories' Quarter Totals. The category names and the totals aren't next to each other, so you'll need to select two separate ranges.

  1. Create the chart: a 2-D Clustered Column chart from A4:A9 and E4:E9. It should have five columns, one per category, and nothing from the other columns.

    6 pts

  2. Move it to a new chart sheet named Sales Chart.

    5 pts

  3. Chart title: Q3 2026 Sales by Category

    4 pts

  4. Data labels: add data labels so each column shows its dollar amount. Any chart style is fine, as long as the labels are readable.

    5 pts

Everything in this part happens on Sheet2. The owner is looking at an $18,500 espresso machine, financed over 5 years at 6.9%. Do the tasks in the order listed. Goal Seek changes the down payment, so it comes last.

  1. Rename Sheet2 to Espresso Loan.

    3 pts

  2. Defined names: select A3:B9 and create names from the labels in the left column. Check the list of names afterwards. You should have seven new ones: Machine_Price, Down_Payment, Annual_Rate, Years, Loan_Amount, Monthly_Payment and Total_Cost.

    5 pts

  3. Loan Amount and Total Cost: using names only, no cell references:

    • In B7, the machine price minus the down payment.
    • In B9, the monthly payment times the number of months, plus the down payment.

    B9 shows 0 until B8 has its formula. That's expected.

    6 pts

  4. Monthly Payment: in B8, use the PMT function, with names for every argument. Payments are monthly, so the rate and the number of periods both need converting. Make the loan amount negative so the payment shows as a positive number.

    7 pts

  5. Number formats: B3:B4 and B7:B9 as Currency, 2 decimal places (Currency, not Accounting). B5 as Percent Style with 1 decimal place (6.9%). A14:A18 as Percent Style with 0 decimal places.

    4 pts

  6. Data table: the owner wants to see the monthly payment at five different interest rates. In B13, enter a formula that refers to the monthly payment (use its name). Then build a one-variable data table on A13:B18 that substitutes each rate in A14:A18 into the Annual Rate cell. Format B13:B18 as Currency, 2 decimal places.

    6 pts

  7. Goal Seek (last): the owner can afford exactly $250 a month. Use Goal Seek to set the monthly payment in B8 to 250 by changing the down payment in B4. Keep the solution. The loan amount, total cost and data table all update on their own.

    4 pts

Last touches, once every formula and format is in place.

  1. AutoFit columns A through H on Q3 Sales, so nothing shows as ##### or gets cut off. Do the same for columns A and B on Espresso Loan.

    3 pts

  2. Freeze panes on Q3 Sales so rows 1 through 4 (the titles and column headings) stay on screen when you scroll down.

    3 pts

  3. Orientation: set Q3 Sales to print in Landscape.

    3 pts

  4. Fit to one page: scale Q3 Sales to 1 page wide by 1 page tall. Check it in Print Preview.

    3 pts

  5. Print area: on Espresso Loan, set the print area to A1:B9, so only the loan summary prints and the data table doesn't.

    3 pts

When you're done, the workbook has three tabs: Q3 Sales, Espresso Loan and Sales Chart. The order of the tabs isn't graded. Click into Q3 Sales and press Ctrl+Home (Mac: Fn+Control+Left Arrow) before you save, so it opens at the top.
