New ideas this week

Last week was the basics of a worksheet. This week adds rules that format cells for you, more number formats, and your first functions besides SUM. The instruction file uses these words without explaining them, so here they are in one place.

The Excel ideas this assignment uses, with the moment in the video where each one comes up.
IdeaWhat it meansIn the video
Conditional formattingFormatting that Excel applies only to cells that meet a rule, such as "this value appears more than once." Change the data and the formatting follows it.
Manage RulesThe list of every conditional formatting rule on the sheet, with the range each one applies to. This is where you delete one rule without touching anything else.
Data barsA conditional format that draws a bar in each cell, sized by its value. Positive values bar to the right, negative to the left.
Deleting a rowRemoves the whole row, and every row below moves up and takes the next number. That's why there's still a row 7 afterwards.
Percent StyleShows a value like 0.0976 as 9.76%. The value underneath doesn't change, only how it's displayed.
Decimal placesHow many digits show after the decimal point. Increase Decimal and Decrease Decimal change it one place per click.
Currency vs. AccountingBoth add a dollar sign. The $ button on the ribbon applies Accounting, which lines the dollar signs up at the left edge of the cell. Currency puts the $ right next to the number, and you pick it from the Number Format drop-down.
Hide and UnhideA hidden column is still there and still in every formula. It just isn't shown. To unhide it, select the columns on both sides of it.
COUNTA function that counts how many cells in a range contain numbers. AutoSum's drop-down calls it Count Numbers.
Order of operationsExcel multiplies and divides before it adds and subtracts, left to right. Parentheses make it do the part inside them first.

Why step 11 needs parentheses

The step wants the difference between the total (I3) and the projection (I5), divided by the projection. In the video the total is about $30,700 and the projection $40,000.

  • =I3-I5/I5 divides first: I5/I5 is 1, so this is 30,700 − 1. As a percentage, that's a huge number. Wrong.
  • =(I3-I5)/I5 subtracts first: −9,300 ÷ 40,000, about −23%. The office raised about 23% less than it projected. Right.

Whenever an instruction says to do one thing and then divide or multiply the result, that's your cue for parentheses.

Four details worth knowing

  • Two worksheets, in order. Steps 1 through 6 happen on the Annual Budget sheet; steps 7 through 11 on the Charitable sheet. Check the tabs at the bottom before you start each group.
  • Delete the row, don't just empty it. Step 1 wants the whole row containing the first duplicate removed. Select it by clicking its row number, then right-click › Delete. The rows below move up, so the totals row moves from 17 to 16 (3:53).
  • If Spelling finishes instantly, it only checked your selection. After unmerging A16, several cells are still selected. Click one empty cell and run Spelling again; when Excel asks whether to continue from the beginning, say Yes (8:16).
  • The $ button is the wrong one for step 8. It applies Accounting. Step 8 asks for Currency, which is in the Number Format drop-down (11:42).

The eleven graded steps

Steps 1–6 are on the Annual Budget worksheet.

  1. 1

    Highlight duplicates in B4:B16, then delete the first duplicate's row

    Use conditional formatting to highlight Duplicate Values in B4 through B16 with Yellow Fill with Dark Yellow Text. One item shows up twice. Delete the entire row that holds its first appearance.

  2. 2

    In E4, budgeted minus actual; fill it to E15

    In E4, enter a formula without a function that subtracts the actual amount (D4) from the budgeted amount (C4). Then fill E5:E15 with it using the fill handle. E4 should show $5,000.

  3. 3

    Clear the conditional formatting from C4:C15

    The blue formatting in C4 through C15 comes from a rule. Remove that rule, and only that rule. The duplicate rule from step 1 stays.

  4. 4

    Percent Style with two decimal places on F4:F16

    Apply Percent Style to F4 through F16, then show two decimal places.

  5. 5

    Blue gradient data bars on E4:E15

    Add a conditional formatting rule to E4 through E15 that uses Gradient Fill › Blue Data Bar. Negative differences bar to the left in red. That's expected.

  6. 6

    Unmerge A16, then spell check the worksheet

    Unmerge cell A16. Then run Spelling on the whole worksheet and correct every misspelling. There are three.

Steps 7–11 are on the Charitable worksheet.

  1. 7

    Fill A1:F1 Teal, Accent 2, Lighter 80%; hide column F

    Give the merged title range A1:F1 the fill color Teal, Accent 2, Lighter 80%. Then hide column F.

  2. 8

    Currency, $ sign, zero decimal places on B4:B12

    Apply the Currency number format (not Accounting) to B4 through B12, with the dollar sign and 0 decimal places.

  3. 9

    SUM the donations in I3

    In I3, enter a formula that uses the SUM function to total B4:B12. AutoSum can't guess the range from I3, so drag across B4 to B12 yourself before pressing Enter.

  4. 10

    COUNT the gifts in I4

    In I4, enter a formula that uses the COUNT function on B4:B12. The answer is 9.

  5. 11

    In I6, (total minus projected) divided by projected

    In I6, enter a formula without a function that subtracts the projected amount (I5) from the total (I3), then divides that result by I5. It needs parentheses. The result is about −23%.

Windows and Mac differences

The steps are the same on both. Almost everything this week is on the Home tab, which looks nearly the same on Mac and Windows. The differences are mostly right-clicking, a few menu names and keyboard shortcuts. Pick your platform.

On Windows

  • Enable Editing. Downloaded files open in Protected View with a yellow bar across the top. Select Enable Editing before anything works.
  • Conditional formatting: Home › Conditional Formatting. Highlight Cells Rules, Data Bars and Manage Rules are all in that one menu.
  • Deleting a row: click the row number, then right-click › Delete.
  • Percent Style, decimals, Currency: the Home tab's Number group. The Number Format drop-down box holds Currency.
  • Unmerge: the small arrow beside Merge & Center › Unmerge Cells.
  • Spelling: Review › Spelling, or F7.
  • Hide and Unhide: right-click the column letter.
  • COUNT: the arrow beside the Σ AutoSum button › Count Numbers.
  • Finding your file: File Explorer, View › Details, then click the Date modified heading to sort newest first.

On Mac

Cengage's material is written for Windows. Most of this assignment matches, but check these before you decide something is broken. I've done these assignments on a Mac myself. If a command genuinely isn't where I say it is, message me through the Canvas Inbox rather than guessing at a substitute.

  • No Protected View. There's no Enable Editing bar. The file opens ready to edit.
  • Right-click is Control-click, or a two-finger click on a trackpad. That's how you reach Delete on a row and Hide or Unhide on a column.
  • Conditional formatting: Home › Conditional Formatting, the same menu. Manage Rules opens a window that lists rules for the current selection or the whole sheet; switch it to This Sheet if you don't see the C4:C15 rule.
  • Percent Style, decimals, Currency: on the Home tab, as on Windows.
  • Unmerge: the arrow beside Merge & Center › Unmerge Cells.
  • Spelling: Review › Spelling.
  • COUNT: the arrow beside AutoSum › Count Numbers.
  • Finding your file: Finder › Downloads, switch to List view, then click Date Modified to sort newest first.
