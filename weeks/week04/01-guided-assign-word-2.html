The assignment, step by step

Four setup items, then the fifteen graded steps, then submitting. Each one says what to change; the time button next to it shows you how. Always go by the instruction file MindTap gave you — the wording below is a summary, and MindTap grades against its own file.

Copy and paste, don't retype. Four steps this week hand you exact text: the comment reply, the footnote, the placeholder name and the website source details. A single typo in any of them loses the whole step. Copy the text out of the instruction file and use Paste Text Only so you don't drag formatting along with it.

The step numbers below are the ones spoken in the video. If your instruction file numbers something differently, go by your file.

Before you start

  1. A

    Download both files

    An instruction file and a start file with your name in it, ending in _1. There are no support files this week. They land in your Downloads folder.

  2. B

    Open the instruction file and read it

    Select Enable Editing, then View › Page Width to make it readable. Keep it open in a second window the whole time — this week you'll be copying text out of it repeatedly.

  3. C

    Open the start file and save it as the _2 version

    Enable Editing, then File › Save As. Leave it in Downloads, change the 1 in the file name to a 2, and save. Everything you do from here goes in the _2 file — that's the one you submit, and the _1 file stays clean in case you want to restart.

  4. D

    Turn on formatting marks, and check that it's your file

    Home › ¶ shows paragraph and space marks, which makes the indent and spacing steps much easier to see. Then scroll to the bottom of the page: it should say the file was created specifically for you, by name. If it doesn't, delete it and download it again.

The fifteen graded steps

  1. 1

    Modify the Normal style

    Right-click Normal in the Styles gallery and choose Modify, then set all four of these:

    • Font Times New Roman
    • Size 12
    • Line spacing Double
    • Spacing After: 0 (no blank space after a paragraph)

    The spacing controls are easier to get right in Format › Paragraph at the bottom of the Modify dialog than in the little buttons on the dialog itself. Modifying the style is the whole point — don't select the text and format it directly, or later steps won't match.

  2. 2

    Apply the modified Normal style to the first four paragraphs

    The MLA heading block at the top: the writer's name, the instructor, the course, and the date ending April 2027. Select all four and click Normal in the Styles gallery, so they pick up the settings from step 1.

  3. 3

    Add the MLA page-number header

    Double-click in the grayed-out header area at the top of the page. On the Header & Footer tab choose Page Number › Top of Page › Plain Number 3. Then type the writer's last name and one space before the page number. Double-click anywhere in the body to close the header.

    That space matters — MLA wants Lastname 1, not Lastname1.

  4. 4

    First-line indent of 0.5" on all body paragraphs

    From the paragraph that begins "As the term … suggests" through the last one, which ends "… makes sense to the viewer." Select them all, open the Paragraph settings dialog (the small arrow at the corner of the Paragraph group), and under Indentation set Special: First line, By: 0.5".

    Use the dialog, not the Tab key. Tabbing each paragraph looks the same on screen and grades as wrong.

  5. 5

    Reply to the comment about the footnote

    One of Professor Garcia's comments asks for a footnote. Reply to that comment with the exact text your instruction file gives — in the video it's I added a footnote — then select Post Reply. Copy the text out of the instruction file and use Paste Text Only rather than typing it.

    Post the reply on the comment that asks for the footnote. That is not the same comment as the one step 11 tells you to delete.

  6. 6

    Insert a footnote defining "histogram"

    Find the sentence that begins "Statistical infographics distill …" and ends "… other types of visual representations." Click after the period, then References › Insert Footnote (the big AB button). Paste the footnote text from your instruction file — in the video it says a histogram groups numbers into ranges — using Paste Text Only.

    Can't find the sentence? Home › Find and type a few words of it. Faster than scrolling, and it's how the video does it.

  7. 7

    Modify the Footnote Text style

    12 point, double line spacing, first line indent 0.5" — the same three settings as the body text.

    Footnote Text isn't in the Styles gallery. Right-click inside the footnote text at the bottom of the page, choose Style, confirm the box says Footnote Text, then Modify. Set the size, use Format › Paragraph for the spacing and indent, then Apply.

  8. 8

    Insert a citation placeholder named Choi

    In the third body paragraph, at the sentence about statistical infographics and a tailor-made design firm: click before the period, then References › Insert Citation › Add New Placeholder and enter Choi as the placeholder name.

    Count the body paragraphs from the start of the report — the heading block at the top doesn't count.

  9. 9

    Insert a citation to the existing Westby source

    In the fourth body paragraph, at the sentence about comparison infographics comparing products or services: click before the period, then References › Insert Citation and pick Westby from the list of sources already in the document. This one is a pick, not a new entry — don't retype it as a new source.

  10. 10

    Replace "present" with a synonym starting with S

    On page two, the sentence beginning "Process infographics present …". Click in the word present, then Review › Thesaurus and scroll for a suggestion that starts with s — show is the one that fits.

    Don't click the word in the Thesaurus pane — that just looks it up again. Hover over it, use the down arrow that appears at its right, and choose Insert. The video gets this wrong first and undoes it.

  11. 11

    Delete a comment, then cite a new website source

    Two things, on the sentence about 65% of viewers:

    • Read the comment attached to that sentence, then delete it (Review › Delete Comment, or the comment's own menu).
    • Click before the period and add a new source from the information in Table 1: References › Insert Citation › Add New Source, Type of Source: Web site, then tick Corporate Author and fill in the rest.

    In the video the values are Design Group / Power of Infographics / published 2027, March, 10 / accessed 2027, April, 20 / Medium: Digital. Take yours from your own Table 1 and read the dialog back before clicking OK — a typo here loses the step.

    Open the instruction file on a second screen or window first. Once the citation dialog is open you can't switch documents to go look something up.

  12. 12

    Edit the Zapata citation to add page 16

    In the next paragraph, right-click the Zapata citation and choose Edit Citation. Put 16 in the Pages box and select OK. Edit Citation, not Edit Source — the page number belongs to this one reference, not to the source itself.

  13. 13

    Delete the Linear source from the source list

    References › Manage Sources, select Linear in the current list, and Delete. It isn't cited in the paper, so it shouldn't appear in the bibliography you build in step 15 — which is why this comes before it.

  14. 14

    Replace every "structure" with "layout"

    Home › Replace. Find what: structure. Replace with: layout. Then Replace All — it should report 2 replacements. If you get a different number, close the dialog and check you didn't leave stray text in either box.

  15. 15

    Insert the bibliography with no built-in heading

    Scroll to the blank paragraph at the very end of the document, below the existing Works Cited heading. Then References › Bibliography and pick Insert Bibliography — the plain command at the bottom of the menu, not the "Bibliography" or "Works Cited" gallery entries above it. Those come with their own heading, and the document already has one.

    Compare the result against the sample in the instruction file. It should list your sources and look identical.

Submitting

  1. E

    Save and close both files

    Save the _2 file, close it, and close the instruction file too. A file still open in Word can upload incomplete.

  2. F

    Upload the _2 file and select Submit

    Drag and drop it into MindTap. Sorting your Downloads folder by Date Modified puts the file you just saved at the top.

  3. G

    Open the Graded Summary Report

    It lists every step with the points it earned. Read it even if you got 25 out of 25 — and if you didn't, it names the step to fix before your next attempt. With fifteen steps and ten attempts, there's no reason to leave points on this one.

Windows and Mac differences

The steps are the same on both, but this week has more real differences than last week's flyer did — citations and the Thesaurus are the two places Mac Word genuinely diverges. Pick your platform.

On Windows

  • Enable Editing. Downloaded files open in Protected View with a yellow bar across the top. You must select Enable Editing before anything works.
  • Formatting marks: Ctrl+Shift+8, or the ¶ button in the Home tab's Paragraph group.
  • Paste Text Only: paste, then use the small clipboard button that appears and choose the A icon. Or Ctrl+Alt+V › Unformatted Text.
  • Managing sources: References › Manage Sources opens the Source Manager, with the master list on the left and this document's list on the right. Delete Linear from the current list.
  • Find and Replace: Ctrl+H. Undo is Ctrl+Z; redo is Ctrl+Y.
  • Finding your file: File Explorer, View › Details, then click the Date modified heading to sort newest first.

On Mac

Cengage's material is written for Windows. Most of this assignment matches, but check these before you decide something is broken. I've done these assignments on a Mac myself — if a command genuinely isn't where I say it is, message me through the Canvas Inbox rather than guessing at a substitute.

  • No Protected View. There's no Enable Editing bar — the file opens ready to edit.
  • Modifying a style: open the Styles Pane from the Home tab, then Control-click (or right-click) Normal › Modify Style. Same dialog, same Format › Paragraph button at the bottom.
  • Formatting marks: ⌘+8.
  • The page number: recent versions of Word for Mac have the same Header & Footer tab and Plain Number 3 gallery. If yours only offers Insert › Page Numbers…, set Position: Top of page and Alignment: Right — that's the same result.
  • Paste Text Only: Edit › Paste Special (⌘+Ctrl+V) › Unformatted Text.
  • Managing sources is the big one. References › Citations opens a Citations pane rather than the Windows Source Manager. The full list lives behind the pane's settings (gear) › Citation Source Manager, and that's where you delete Linear. Edit Citation for the Zapata page number is on the citation's own drop-down in the document, same as Windows.
  • Thesaurus: Review › Thesaurus, or Tools › Thesaurus. The list works the same way — hover a word, use its arrow, choose Insert.
  • Find and Replace: ⌘+Shift+H, or Edit › Find › Replace…. Undo is ⌘+Z; redo is ⌘+Y.
  • Finding your file: Finder › Downloads, switch to List view, then click Date Modified to sort newest first.

Since this was recorded

A few things will look different on your screen than they do on mine. Where this page and the video disagree, this page is right.

  • This is take three. The audio didn't record the first time, so by the time you see me I already have several Graded Summary Reports and a submitted file sitting there. Your list will be empty the first time through — that's normal. (Video 0:21 and 21:53.)
  • I post the comment reply out of order. I do step 5's reply at 7:15, before the footnote exists, and then at 16:12 I realize it probably belonged with the step 11 comment. The grader only cares that the right comment carries the right reply and that the step 11 comment is deleted — so follow your instruction file's order rather than mine. (Video 7:15 and 16:12.)
  • The duplicate source in my list. At 19:21 I have two "Power of Infographics" sources because one of my earlier attempts had a typo in it. You should have exactly one. If you see duplicates, delete the wrong one before you insert the bibliography.
  • Sentences and names, as spoken. I read several of the sentences, source names and the footnote text out loud, and the audio garbles a few of them. Go by your instruction file for the exact wording of every sentence, name, date and footnote — and copy-paste rather than typing.
  • The dates belong to the scenario, not to us. April 2027, March 10 2027 and the April 20 2027 access date are part of the assignment's fictional research paper. They have nothing to do with our semester — enter them exactly as Table 1 and the instruction file give them.
  • Module release. Modules post on the Friday before the week begins, so Week 4 opened Friday 9/18. You can start early.

Using AI on this assignment

The course AI policy is Limited. You can ask an AI tool to explain what a command does, to tell you where something lives on the Mac ribbon when the video shows Windows, to troubleshoot an error, or to quiz you afterward. You build the Word file yourself. Spell check, grammar check and the Thesaurus are ordinary Word features and are always allowed — step 10 is asking you to use one.

The practical reason: the 200-point Word Project in Week 6 assumes you can insert a citation, modify a style and build a bibliography from memory. Watching someone else's paper get formatted is not the same as formatting one.
