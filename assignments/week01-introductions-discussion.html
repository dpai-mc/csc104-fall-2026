What you are doing and why

This course is fully online and asynchronous, which means we will never all be in a room together. That makes it very easy to spend fifteen weeks next to twenty-five people you never meet. This discussion is how we fix that. It also gives me a first read on where everyone is starting from, so I can aim the weekly walkthroughs at the right level.

The assignment in one sentence

Post one introduction of about 150–250 words answering the five prompts below, then write a substantive reply of about 50 words or more to two different classmates.

How to complete it

  1. Draft your introduction

    Use the Write your post tab. It walks you through the five prompts one at a time, counts your words as you go, and assembles a finished post you can copy and paste into Canvas.

  2. Post it to the Canvas discussion by Friday 9/4

    In the Canvas discussion board for this assignment, choose Reply on the main discussion topic, paste your introduction, and post it. Posting on Friday rather than Sunday matters — your classmates need something to reply to.

  3. Read what other people wrote

    Come back Saturday or Sunday and read through the board. Look for people whose situation, goals, or worries sound like yours, and for people whose experience is different from yours.

  4. Reply to two classmates by Sunday 9/6

    Use the Reply to classmates tab for what makes a reply count. Reply to two different people, and reply directly beneath their post so they get the notification.

  5. Check your work against the checklist

    The Grading & checklist tab has the exact rubric I grade with and a checklist you can tick off before you close the tab.

What your post needs to cover

Five things. Nothing here is personal beyond what you choose to share.

The five prompts
#PromptWhat I'm looking for
1 What should we call you? The name you actually go by, and how to say it if it is often mispronounced.
2 Your program or major, and where you are headed Degree, certificate, transfer plan, a job you are aiming at — or "still deciding," which is a real answer.
3 Why you are taking CSC 104 and what you want out of it Required for your program? Job asked for it? One specific thing you want to be able to do by December.

A note on AI for this assignment

Per the course AI policy, write this one yourself. An introduction is your own voice by definition, and an AI-written introduction to a class of people you are about to spend fifteen weeks with is an odd way to start. Spell check and grammar check are ordinary application features and are always fine.

Replying to two classmates

Ten of the twenty-five points are for your two replies, and they are the part students most often lose points on — not because the replies are bad, but because "Welcome to the class!" is not a reply. Aim for about 50 words or more each, to two different people, posted beneath their introduction so they are notified.

A reply that earns the points

  • Names something specific from their post.
  • Connects it to something real — your own experience, your program, a shared worry.
  • Ends with a question, an offer, or a genuinely useful piece of information.
  • Sounds like a person talking to a person.

A reply that does not

  • "Nice to meet you, good luck this semester!"
  • A summary of what they already said, with nothing added.
  • A reply that would fit under literally anyone's post.
  • Two replies posted under the same person.

Sentence starters, if you are stuck

  • Shared situation: "You mentioned you are juggling this with a full-time job — I am too, and I am planning to do my coursework on…"
  • Shared goal: "We are both headed toward … Did you already take …?"
  • Different starting point: "You said Excel is the one you are dreading. I use it at work for …, and the thing that finally made it click for me was …"
  • Curiosity: "You said you … — how did you get into that?"
  • Offer: "I am also in the … program — if you want someone to compare notes with on the Word Project in Week 6, I am in."

Common point losses

  • Skipping a prompt. Prompt 4 — your experience with the applications — is the one most often left out, and it is the one I use to aim the course.
  • Both replies under the same person. They must be two different classmates.
  • One-line replies. "Welcome!" is friendly and earns nothing.
  • Posting everything Sunday night. If nobody posts until Sunday, nobody has anything to reply to.

What a full-credit post looks like

These are made up, but they are the shape of what earns the points. Do not copy them — copy the structure.

Full credit · 15 / 15

Hi everyone, I'm Marisol — most people call me Mari, and it rhymes with "starry."

I'm in my second year at Mendocino, working toward the Business Administration AS-T and planning to transfer to Sonoma State in fall 2028. Long term I want to run the office side of my family's vineyard operation, which right now runs on paper and my uncle's memory.

CSC 104 is required for my degree, but I would honestly be taking it anyway. The specific thing I want by December is to be able to build a spreadsheet that adds itself up — right now I retype totals into a calculator and then type them back in, and I know that is not how this is supposed to work.

Experience-wise: I use Word regularly for papers, though I suspect I do everything the slow way. Excel I have used a little, mostly typing numbers into boxes. PowerPoint I have never really used beyond one high school project.

Outside of class I work about 25 hours a week at a tasting room in Hopland, and I have a very opinionated three-legged cat named Waffle. I do my coursework Tuesday and Thursday mornings, so if you are looking for someone to compare notes with in that window, say hi.

That post is about 210 words and answers all five prompts. Notice how many hooks it leaves for someone else to reply to: the vineyard, the transfer plan, the Excel worry, the tasting room, the cat.
